Scope of this draft

This draft describes the NotiLog app source reviewed on 20 October 2026. It is prepared for David Holtschke’s review and is not an approved public policy or a legal compliance assurance.

The reviewed app has no NotiLog account, custom backend or built-in cloud sync. No advertising, analytics, purchase or third-party crash-reporting SDK integration was found in the reviewed project configuration. Source review alone does not establish the behavior of a future release, App Store settings or an email provider.

Data on your device

NotiLog stores notification information passed to it by your configured automations: title, subtitle and message text; source-app name and identifier; capture dates, repeated-capture records and time-sensitive status; and your pin choices. Settings and cached app metadata/artwork are also stored locally.

The archive uses local SwiftData storage with CloudKit disabled in the reviewed configuration. Notification content is not sent to a developer-operated server by the reviewed capture, storage or backup code.

Notification content can contain personal or sensitive information. Choose which source apps to capture and protect access to your device. This draft does not claim that all local data or manual backups are separately encrypted by NotiLog.

Capture and permissions

You choose source apps and enable their notification automations in Apple Shortcuts. Those automations pass the notification fields to NotiLog’s Archive Notification action. Disable or remove an automation in Shortcuts to stop its future captures; existing archived data remains until deleted.

The app cannot reconstruct notifications from before capture was configured. What is archived depends on what the configured automation passes to the app.

Optional App Lock

App Lock asks the system to authenticate with Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode. NotiLog receives an authentication result, not your biometric template or passcode. App Lock protects the interface; it does not encrypt manual backup files or disable the capture automation.

David’s review: Confirm all permission prompts, shortcut behavior and authentication paths against the shipping build and hardware. Do not describe the App Lock setting as a guarantee that capture or exported files are blocked.

Apple services and network use

App metadata and icons

To show source-app names, icons and App Store links, the reviewed app requests public metadata from Apple’s App Store lookup service. The request includes a source app’s bundle identifier and a storefront country code, with fallback lookups where needed. These requests reveal the queried app identifier and storefront to Apple. Notification titles, subtitles and message text are not included in the lookup request.

The app downloads artwork from the URL returned by Apple and caches it locally. This network activity is separate from storing the notification archive. Apple’s handling of its service requests is described in Apple’s privacy information.

Setup and links

Opening the shared shortcut during setup uses Apple’s iCloud Shortcuts service. Opening an App Store link or support email uses the selected system app or service. These user-directed actions are not a built-in cloud sync for the notification archive.

David’s review: Recheck the final app’s network dependencies and any Apple diagnostics, App Store metrics or external support tools used outside the app code. Do not infer “no network requests” or a final App Store privacy label from the absence of an analytics SDK.

Retention and deletion

The reviewed main app offers 7 Days, 30 Days, 90 Days and Forever. Finite cleanup removes unpinned notifications older than the selected period, based on each notification’s latest capture date. Pinned notifications are kept by retention cleanup; Forever has no age cutoff.

Cleanup runs during app activity and capture processing, so this draft does not promise deletion at an exact background deadline. Settings previews eligible removals before applying a shorter period when records would be deleted, with an option to export first. Manual deletion of a notification removes all its repeated captures; Delete All History removes archived notifications and source-app records after confirmation.

Deleted records cannot be recovered by changing retention back to a longer period. An earlier backup would be needed, if available.

David’s review: The separate retention safety fix preserves existing Forever/finite choices and treats unreadable preferences conservatively. Verify its integration into the actual release before describing upgrade behavior as shipped. This draft does not claim that the proposed fix can recover past deletions.

Manual backups and sync

Exported backups contain readable notification content, source-app information and backup metadata such as app/schema version and export date. The reviewed exporter writes JSON data and does not encrypt the exported file.

You choose a destination in Files. Saving to iCloud Drive or another cloud provider sends the file to that provider under its own terms and privacy practices. App Lock does not protect a copy after export. Delete exported copies separately when you no longer want them.

NotiLog does not provide built-in cloud sync in the reviewed source. Device-level backups may include local app data depending on your system and provider settings; confirm those settings separately.

Support contact

The proposed NotiLog contact is contact@holtsdav.com. If you choose to email, the address, message and any attachments you send leave the local app and are handled through the email services involved. Please use synthetic examples and do not send your notification archive or private message content.

David’s review: Confirm the operator identity, contact address, email provider, support-message retention and request-handling process before this becomes a public policy. These details cannot be established from the app source.
